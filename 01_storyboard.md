# Main Storyboard and Presenter Cues

Target: approximately 4½ minutes inside a 30-minute panel. The presenter advances each reveal; no timer forces a move past a discussion pause. The audience sees ten narrative beats, while the implementation can use several small states within each beat.

| Beat | What lands on stage | Presenter idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. AI helps humans | “AI HELPS HUMANS SUPERVISE”; three synthetic source counts; human still chooses next action | “At this stage AI helps people supervise.” |
| 2. AI observes | “27 → 42”; liquidity pattern changed | “Sentinel observed without waiting to be prompted.” |
| 3. Agent investigates | “42 → 61”; Investigator reveals its chosen checks | “I didn't tell it which query to run.” |
| 4. Agent expansion | “ONE OBJECTIVE”; branching graph; then “8 AGENTS”; then “27 SUB-TASKS” | “One task does not mean one agent action.” |

| 6. AI supervises AI | “486 EVENTS”; then “1 MATERIAL ISSUE”; linked causal chain; two-question human decision; three distinct loops | “Humans belong at boundaries of authority, not every boundary of computation.” |
| 7. Challenge and trust | “WHO SUPERVISES THE SUPERVISORY AI?”; three stage trust questions; Bank Compliance challenge; “SUPERVISORY FACT: CONTESTED” | “The attention allocator must be inspectable and challengeable.” |

## Human-in-the-Right-Loop choreography

1. **Reassurance:** Show three oversight assurances at audience-readable scale.
2. **Branching:** Draw Sentinel → Investigator → EntityGraph, HistoricalTransactions, DisclosureReview, CounterpartyCheck and PolicyMapper, with Interpreter and parallel functions. The eight-agent count covers active instances during the burst; Supervisory AI and Bank Compliance enter later.
3. **Flood:** Replay exactly 486 interleaved events across 3.2 simulated seconds. Ordinary retrieval, relationship, fingerprint, confidence, policy, tool and trace events surround the real EntityGraph/T-17 classification change. The downstream risk update 39 → 76 also exists in the trace. Do not highlight T-17 while it passes.
4. **Naive review:** Clear the screen and ask a person to AUTHORISE or REJECT 486 events. Both controls are clickable, but neither records approval. Either response reveals “ON WHAT BASIS?”
5. **Compression:** Show 486 alone, then one issue alone. Only after a pause reveal T-17 → Investigator score 39 → 76 → Interpreter restriction recommendation → runtime mandate boundary.
6. **Right-loop decision:** Show two questions: whether Entity X was correctly reclassified, and if valid whether intervention is justified. The answer remains pending during the timed main path. Original sources, uncertainty, disagreement, challenge and review controls are available in detail.
7. **Second-order trust:** The Supervisory AI selects what a human sees. Stage questions are provenance, uncertainty and contestability. The detail drawer exposes all eight controls and its flag/escalate-only mandate. Bank Compliance may challenge the derived classification without rewriting source evidence.

Score continuity: 27 → 42 → provisional 61 while entity context is unknown; a source check resolves a central-bank-related baseline of 39; T-17's derived reclassification raises it 39 → 76; the later counterfactual restores 39. Each change has an attributed event, even if only the decisive transitions dominate the stage.

## Three loops

- **Machine activity:** routine queries, retrieval, tool calls, scoring, evidence processing and agent-to-agent work at machine speed.
- **Supervisory control:** separate simulated AI watches agent behavior, conflicts, material state changes and mandate boundaries, then surfaces linked issues.
- **Human authority:** people judge material intervention, irreversible action, legal/regulatory determination, mandate expansion, exceptional override, policy change and systemic risk.

The final question is whether humans can trust, challenge and reconstruct the system that decided what they needed to look at. The demo does not claim that adding another AI automatically solves oversight.
