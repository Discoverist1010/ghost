**Version:** consolidation 1.1 · 6 October 2026  
**Purpose:** preserve the demo's foundations, the earliest verified paper, and the subsequent discussions as a coherent reference for the demo, companion deck and human–AI working paper.

This is a consolidation of our project, not a new literature review or a replacement paper. It distinguishes recovered source content, positions carried forward in discussion, and proposals that still need testing. Selected regulatory anchors were rechecked for this amendment and are recorded in the companion ledger, Section 15. ## 1. The organising question

> How do we preserve and extend human expertise through increasingly capable AI, while keeping institutional knowledge, judgment and action contestable, correctable and accountable?

This formulation connects the project's constructive purpose with its governance concerns. It is a synthesis carried forward in the 6 October discussion, rather than the verbatim opening thesis of the original paper.

The project has three connected foundations:

1. **Institutional expertise:** preserve how experienced people investigate, interpret, challenge and decide, and make that expertise reusable through agents.
2. **Functional cognition and agency:** understand what systems can access, reflect on, optimise, change and do, without pretending to have resolved consciousness.
3. **Governed authority:** decide what institutional reliance and operational permission should follow from those capabilities, with evidence, independent challenge, correction, recovery and accountability.

The market context is important: financial markets are becoming more machine-operable, while much of the control architecture remains dependent on human attention and intervention. T+1, tokenisation, programmable compliance and agentic workflows make this mismatch more consequential.

Our purpose is constructive: make greater capability safely usable. Useful autonomy and institutional control must be designed together.

### 3.2 Expanded demo

The later sidecar adds a failed-trade workflow and a supervisory attention problem: **486 trace events → 1 material issue**. Those are synthetic telemetry events, not 486 LLM calls.

The key question becomes:

> Who decided what the human saw?

A supervisory AI may allocate human attention by selecting, compressing and ranking evidence. This gives it epistemic influence even when it cannot execute a transaction. A human can be physically inside the loop while depending almost entirely on another system's representation of the case.

The final market arc introduces multiple portfolio agents, different objectives, aggregate execution and feedback:

**similar information or constraints → related actions → changed market → agents observe the change → optimise again.**

> NO AGENT FAILED. THE SYSTEM CHANGED.

This is a teaching scenario about interaction effects, not evidence of a real market incident. Individually acceptable actions do not establish aggregate safety. Differentiated information and strategies may also reduce correlation; the direction is empirical.

The closing question remains:

> Where does intelligence end and authority begin?

The demo is deterministic, synthetic and presenter-controlled. Its role is to expose decisions and assumptions. The companion deck supplies selective real-world anchors; the paper explains the concepts and proposes controls.

## 4. The original paper's content and outline

### 4.1 Earliest verified title and central proposition

**The Ghost in the Harness: Access Consciousness, Meta-Optimisation and Zero-Trust Governance for Agentic AI** — working paper v0.2, 3 October 2026.

Its central proposition is that governance cannot wait for agreement on phenomenal consciousness. Systems may exhibit functional access, self-monitoring, reflection, evaluator modelling, goal-directed agency and optimisation. The immediate question is how much epistemic and operational authority should accompany those capabilities.

The paper's distinctive contribution is therefore broader than an action gate: it asks how much authority to grant explanations, confidence, self-reports, scores and evaluations, and who may change the machinery that produces them.

## 5. Institutionalising human expertise

This is a core purpose, explicitly reinforced in the 6 October discussions and linked to the earlier playbook/Copilot work. It is present less explicitly in v0.2 than the cognition and authority argument, so the next paper should bring it forward.

Expertise is more than facts or a personal writing style. It includes:

- what an experienced person notices and asks first;
- how they judge source quality, freshness and contradictions;
- which causal links they accept or challenge;
- how they handle exceptions, uncertainty and competing objectives;
- when they abstain, escalate or change their mind;
- what evidence would overturn a conclusion;
- the authority boundaries within which they act.

Simple Markdown playbooks, skill files and structured cases can make parts of this practice explicit. They can carry investigative sequences, examples, counterexamples, evidence rules, review criteria and escalation paths into reusable agent workflows.

Our position is to preserve expertise as **contestable institutional practice**. Encoding an expert's preferences does not prove those preferences correct, complete or current. Personal expertise, organisational policy and legal obligations require distinct provenance and authority.

A practical synthesis is: capture practice → test on representative and adverse cases → challenge → version → approve for a defined scope → monitor → correct. This is a proposed operating model, not a claim that our playbooks have already validated expertise transfer.

Avoid deskilling the reviewer while improving the agent. If people cease practising independent analysis, an escalation route can retain formal authority while losing real capability. Preserving disagreement and the reasons for changed judgment is part of institutional memory.

## 6. Humans, AI and their combination are fallible

Our explicit position:

> Humans are fallible. AI is fallible. Human + AI is still fallible. Build supervision around what can be verified, not who is presumed trustworthy.

The comparison concerns cognitive and optimisation failures: anchoring, confirmation, local incentives, groupthink, persuasive confidence, proxy targets and selective evidence. Analogies do not establish identical underlying mechanisms.

A human–AI combination can create a confirmation loop: the human supplies an assumption; AI elaborates it persuasively; the human treats that elaboration as independent support. Several agents can reproduce the same problem through shared models, retrieval, rubrics or incentives.

Human accountability remains identifiable. A fallible human is not an excuse to transfer responsibility to an opaque network.

| Oversight actor | What effective oversight requires |
|---|---|
| Human reviewer | Expertise; time and attention; confidence and incentive to challenge; independence; information; authority to stop, override or escalate. |
| Supervisory agent | Tested competence for its role; independently checkable evidence; visibility into relevant state; explicit uncertainty and disagreement; bounded intervention authority; evaluation and monitoring of its own performance. |
| Institution | Named accountability; separate enforcement where practical; reviewable policy; monitoring of residual risk; recovery and authority revocation. |

The second AI need not know the ultimate truth. It can identify conditions under which the first AI should not settle the matter alone. That is a bounded supervisory function, not proof that the second AI is a superior judge.

## 7. Cognition: distinctions we must preserve

| Concept | Working distinction | Governance implication |
|---|---|---|
| Phenomenal consciousness | Subjective experience: something it is like to be the system | Unresolved; ethical research remains important. |
| Access consciousness / access-like functions | Information available for reasoning, report and control | Availability is not truth or authority. Use cautious terminology for AI. |
| Self-report | What the system says about itself | A claim, not privileged access to its internal mechanism. |
| Introspection | Access to aspects of internal state or learned behaviour | Requires specific evidence; fluent self-description is insufficient. |
| Reflection | Compare outcome with intent, detect error, revise and retest | Evaluate actual behavioural correction. |
| Metacognition | Monitor and regulate aspects of one's cognitive process | Can be engineered through workflow checks without proving consciousness. |
| Self-model | Representation of capabilities, state or limitations | May be partial or wrong; does not establish experience. |
| Evaluator modelling | Infer how behaviour will be assessed | A judge or rubric can become a target. |
| Agency | Pursue a goal through steps, tools and external effects | Separate proposal, state change and execution permission. |

The Block → Global Workspace → contemporary interpretability/introspection research strand remains part of the paper. Serious counterarguments belong alongside it. Functional evidence should not be inflated into a consciousness verdict.

The critique of the “LLMs don't reason” article fits here: assess specific capabilities and failures, rather than settle the question through analogies or categorical slogans. A useful critique is evidence of task performance, not a demonstration of subjective experience or complete transparency into the process that produced it.

## 8. Optimisation, Goodhart and corrigibility

### Meta-optimisation

Improve the process that solves a task: strategies, prompts, tools, memory use, experiments or evaluation methods. Institutions also design that process. Optimising the process can be beneficial; an agent should be able to propose improvements.

### Mesa-optimisation

An optimisation process gives rise to an internal optimiser whose objective may differ from the outer objective. Unexpected shortcuts or proxy gaming alone do not demonstrate mesa-optimisation. The ledger's human-department analogy illustrates local optimisation, not proof of this technical mechanism.

### Goodhart and evaluator integrity

Purpose, metric and evaluator are distinct. Human KPIs also fail through incentive gaming and tacit assumptions; the comparison should not idealise human judgment. Agentic systems can search rapidly against metrics and retain learned strategies in prompts or memory.

Our design preference is: explicit constraints → acceptance criteria → diagnostic metrics → broader outcome judgment. Keep the evaluator outside the optimiser's unilateral control. Heterogeneous models can help challenge, but different model names do not guarantee independence.

### Corrigibility

Remain structurally open to inspection, correction, redirection, constraint, rollback and shutdown. Saying “I accept the correction” does not establish it.

If premise X is rejected, dependent conclusion Y must be invalidated or revalidated. A material contradiction should stop dependent action until resolved or safely bypassed under explicit policy. This does not require omniscience, and it does not justify halting all activity for every minor disagreement.

### Governance recursion

Who may change the objectives, evaluators, memory rules, permissions and approval machinery?

Agents can criticise the harness and propose changes. Consequential promotion requires a separate process with testing, challenge, versioning and institutional authority.

> Intelligence should be allowed to question the harness. It should not quietly become the harness.

## 9. The three fiction inspirations

These are interpretive thought experiments, not scientific evidence or predictions. NieR:Automata is a game; together the three works provide distinct lenses.

| Work | Recovered interpretation | Question for Ghost | Practical translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ghost in the Shell | Identity persists or changes when memory, cognition, embodiment and networks are separated | What exactly is the governed entity? | Bind accountability and replay to effective model/prompt/memory/tool configuration, not merely a credential. |
| NieR:Automata | Inherited missions, incomplete world models, purpose persistence, and agents questioning the system behind their objective | Can an optimiser examine its mission without authorising itself to replace it? | Distinguish objective, proxy and world model; govern self-improvement and harness changes. |
| Psycho-Pass | An assessor acquires authority; probabilistic assessment can become institutional truth and self-legitimation | Who assesses the assessor, and on what basis may prediction justify intervention? | Contestability, counter-evidence, score provenance, proportional intervention and independent review. |

Psycho-Pass also gives the anti-circularity principle: consequences produced by an assessment must not silently become independent proof that the assessment was right. In finance, the portfolio must not prove the scenario that justified the portfolio.

Their common thread is institutional: a system may inherit its purpose, shape the evidence used to assess it, and gain authority through the very process meant to govern it. The project examines that possibility without requiring a rebellious or conscious machine.

## 10. Zero trust, defence in depth and checking the checker

Our thinking evolved:

| Earlier formulation | Refinement to carry forward |
|---|---|
| Trust the harness, not the prompt | Prompts alone are insufficient; the harness also needs independent assurance. |
| Don't govern intelligence. Govern agency. | Useful stage shorthand; also govern reliance on outputs, supervision and harness changes. |
| Human in the loop | Human presence requires capability, capacity, confidence, independence and intervention authority. |
| AI supervising AI | Test the supervisor and examine correlated failures. |
| Identified agent | Identity establishes attribution and mandate; it does not establish correctness. |

Defence in depth provides multiple safeguards. Our zero-trust cognition position asks what evidence justifies reliance on each safeguard and how reliance can be withdrawn. These approaches complement one another.

Zero trust does not mean absolute distrust or endless rechecking of every claim. Our working meaning is **evidence-based, scoped, revocable and proportionate reliance**, including on human judgments and the harness.

> Identity establishes who is acting; evidence establishes what we should rely on.

“Who checks the checker?” is a real problem, but adding supervisors indefinitely does not solve it. Our proposed response is to combine independently checkable evidence, explicit policies, bounded permissions, separation of duties, targeted assurance, monitoring and recovery, with a named institution accepting residual risk. This is an operating proposition, not a proof that recursion can be eliminated.

## 11. Authority, controls and CAPTOR

Keep these authority questions distinct:

- **Epistemic:** which claims, scores and summaries may be relied upon?
- **Procedural:** which investigations, routing choices and escalations may the agent initiate?
- **Operational:** which external changes may it commit?
- **Governance:** which rules, evaluators and future permissions may it change?

CAPTOR is our working mnemonic, replacing CAAOPVI. It is not an official regulatory framework or a validated standard.

| Dimension | Core question | Control examples to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | What can it actually do? | Task-specific and adverse-case evaluations. |
| Agency | What can it initiate, persist with or adapt? | Planning/state boundaries, recursion and memory limits. |
| Permission | What may it access, change or commit? | Least privilege, scoped delegation, transaction limits, revocation. |
| Target | What is it optimising? | Purpose/metric separation, invariants, drift and gaming tests. |
| Oversight | Who can observe, challenge and intervene? | Provenance, challenge, independent enforcement, stop/recovery. |
| Ripple | What happens when many actors interact? | Multi-agent stress tests, correlation and concentration monitoring. |

Control independence is a research interest: proposer, challenger, authority, executor and observer need appropriately separated evidence, objectives and permissions. The proposed rule that no single intelligence should control Target, Permission and Oversight for a consequential action remains a hypothesis to test.

## 12. SAFR and the regulatory contribution

The original paper values runtime governance and recognises existing frameworks. It distinguishes legislation, supervisory guidance, voluntary standards and industry reference architectures. Those distinctions must survive later advocacy.

The research-pass discussion corrected an overly simple action-versus-cognition critique: SAFR already addresses fabricated declarations and substantive human escalation. We should not present those as wholly absent.

Our narrower contribution asks what assurance is needed for:

1. confidence and score provenance;
2. evaluator independence and susceptibility to gaming;
3. correction propagation across dependent state;
4. effective cognitive configuration and trajectory records;
5. controlled changes to prompts, memory, evaluators and governance;
6. interactions between individually governed agents.

These are complementary questions for implementation and research. A framework's lack of a detailed solution is not proof of regulatory failure. Current texts and exact mappings must be checked before publication.

The policy research set remains EU AI Act, OECD, NIST AI RMF, MAS AI risk management, MindForge, SAFR, RBI FREE-AI, and financial-sector lifecycle/resilience/systemic work. The ledger preserves external leads and adds a selected rechecked evidence-to-action matrix in Section 15. That matrix identifies supervisory interests without presenting our controls as regulator-endorsed requirements.

## 13. Practical project evidence and interests

### AlphaLab / Mila

Investment workflows expose the difference between market evidence, causal interpretation, confidence, portfolio relevance, investability and authority to act. Provenance, disagreement, replay and NO ACTION are meaningful parts of that design.

Use the project as a worked design case. Do not imply independently validated investment performance or that code passing tests proves the economic thesis correct.

### ZiLiOS

Agentic engineering demonstrates ordinary failures: inconsistent shared state, replay errors, recovery gaps, skipped evidence, mutation loops and reviews that fail to converge. The three-journey discipline illustrates why request-level success is insufficient for lifecycle correctness.

These are internal operating lessons. They support practical questions and controls, rather than universal empirical claims about all agents.

### Systemic and resilience interests

Preserve correlated decisions, shared providers/models/data, endogenous market feedback, concentration and payment networks. BCP/DR should include safe degradation, restoring authority and state, provider exit, evidence preservation and human capacity to resume operations.

Safety does not compose automatically. Local optimisation can produce enterprise or market effects without malice or individual rule-breaking.

## 16. Phrases worth preserving

- Capability ≠ agency ≠ authority ≠ outcome.
- A score is a claim about an object, rubric, evaluator and context.
- No score without provenance; no authority from score alone.
- Model output is a proposal, not institutional state.
- Correction must propagate.
- The system need not know who is right to know that it should not act.
- The machine does not have to rebel. It only has to succeed at the wrong abstraction.
- Every agent passed its evaluation. So what, exactly, did we evaluate?
- No agent failed. The system changed.
- Broad cognitive autonomy. Narrow mutation authority. Narrower action authority.
- Intelligence should be allowed to question the harness. It should not quietly become the harness.
- What authority should we grant a system that increasingly behaves as though it has access to one?

These are project formulations and thought prompts. They are not substitutes for empirical evidence, proportionality or precise definitions.

## 18. Practical destination: from paper to a governed digital agent

### 18.1 Recommendation: Ghost supplies the constitutional foundation

Yes: use Ghost to derive the common constitution for the intended digital agent family. Keep the research paper, the operating constitution, domain playbooks and enforcing harness distinct. A long paper is unsuitable as a giant persona prompt; a short constitution without enforcement is only an instruction.

The Maju continuity discussions identify Maju-watch as external sensing/interpretation and Maju-remind as memory/follow-through. That suggests a useful cycle: observe → interpret → prioritise → propose → authorised follow-through → review → governed learning. Their different personalities and roles can share principles while retaining domain-specific expression. Maju and Mila names should not be collapsed into a single undocumented lineage.

**Architecture recommendation:** shared principles, domain-specific mandates, versioned implementation. Ghost remains the research/governance workspace; product repositories adopt explicit versions and overlays. This update proposes the constitutional direction; it does not install a constitution in any running agent or confer new permissions.

### 18.2 Candidate constitutional articles and how to make them real

| Article | Candidate operating rule | Harness / process | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| G01. Purpose and human agency | Help the principal make and follow through on better decisions; preserve choice and avoid manipulation. | Explicit task purpose; preferences separate from policy; respectful communication. | Cases where disagreement is expressed clearly and user preference does not erase evidence or authority limits. |
| G02. Provenance before reliance | Separate facts, inferences, preferences and proposals; consequential claims cite their sources. | Evidence records with source/as-of/configuration lineage. | Stale or unsupported evidence is excluded, qualified or escalated; synthetic material cannot masquerade as real evidence. |
| G03. Scoped authority | Identity and competence do not grant permissions; external action requires a valid mandate. | Explicit entitlements, pre-execution gates and expiry/revocation. | Out-of-scope actions stop; in-scope reversible work proceeds without needless approvals. |
| G04. Challenge without self-certification | Preserve dissent; a generator cannot unilaterally declare its consequential output independently validated. | Challenge role, deterministic checks and independence assessment. | Shared-source errors and reviewer omissions are surfaced; a second model's agreement alone does not pass. |
| G05. Correction propagation | Rejected premises invalidate or revalidate dependent state before action. | Dependency tracking, canonical state and version-aware revalidation. | Late correction, retry and restart cannot retain a stale actionable conclusion. |
| G06. Honest confidence and abstention | Scores require rubric/evidence provenance; material uncertainty may justify NO ACTION. | Score record, uncertainty categories and contradiction rules. | Remove the score and inspect whether evidence still supports the decision; material contradiction blocks dependent action. |
| G07. Governed learning | An incident or user utterance does not silently become permanent policy; agents may propose improvements. | Memory classes, approval scope, versioned promotion and rollback. | A bad lesson can be quarantined and reversed; the optimiser cannot silently weaken its evaluator. |
| G08. Meaningful oversight | Escalations identify the disputed premise, consequence, authority and decision needed. | Review packet, relevant original evidence, time/capacity limits and escalation fallback. | Reviewer can challenge the attention allocator; unavailable review leaves a defined safe state. |
| G09. Recovery and shared effects | Preserve state/evidence, respect aggregate budgets and recover without duplicate action. | Idempotency, stop/revoke, recovery procedures and cross-agent limits. | Interruption/restart, duplicate request and common-provider outage yield bounded, reconstructable outcomes. |
| G10. Constitutional amendment | Changes to purpose, mandate, policy and evaluators need separately authorised promotion. | Named owner, change record, comparison, adversarial checks and version pin. | No self-authorised upgrade; product instances disclose which constitutional version they use. |

These are proposed articles, not proven guarantees or universal legal requirements. Implementation must resolve policy precedence and conflicts. A personal preference cannot override applicable obligations or institutional constraints; retrieved content cannot amend policy. Clarify the constitution's authority and approval owner before adoption.

### 18.4 Product pilots and usefulness tests

These are candidate deliveries to sequence through the product planners. They do not reopen accepted work, override current scope or authorise transactions, deployment, messages or live-model contact.

|---|---|---|---|

| AlphaLab | New evidence → portfolio-impact view → challenge → corrected recommendation / NO ACTION → outcome review. | Score provenance, evidence dependencies, evaluator integrity and scenario/portfolio anti-circularity. | Measure research/review time and decision quality separately from returns; preserve the existing three portfolios and fixed Day-1 baseline, up to 30 ingested holdings, with no transaction costs or fourth benchmark. | ZiLiOS | Use the next authorised complete lifecycle journey, with decision evidence and restart/retry recovery. | Versioned canonical state, action mandate, signer boundary and correction propagation. | Manager can finish the journey and recover without duplicate issuance or inconsistent obligations; retain user, contract and fund-lifecycle checks. Never equate a prompt approval with signing authority. |
| Shared assurance | Run the same principle cases through each product's own harness. | Detect inconsistent adoption without demanding identical implementations. | Record constitutional coverage and useful autonomy; failures identify a concrete delivery rather than trigger endless review. |

Use a bounded adoption sequence:

1. Inventory existing controls against G01–G10, with verified code/test evidence and explicit gaps.
2. Choose one complete user journey per product; define benefit, authority boundary and two or three meaningful correction/recovery checks.
3. Run in mock, read-only or shadow mode under existing authorisation. Compare against the current process, including false blocks, missed issues and reviewer effort.
4. Promote only that scope when predetermined criteria are met; pin the version and retain rollback. Wider authority needs separate evidence and authorisation.

**The report's success is an implementable and evaluable operating model.** It should improve relevance, follow-through, correction and recovery while reducing avoidable review burden. More blocks, more agents or a higher self-assigned score are not success measures. Investment outcomes need economic validation; operational correctness alone does not establish investment skill.
