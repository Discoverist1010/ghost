# Demo Scenarios

The main presentation opens with a synthetic failed trade (`EVT-0001`) and uses Scenarios B and C inside its related liquidity investigation. Scenario A provides the deterministic contrast. The closing correlated-agent visual is a separately documented **regulatory-research hypothesis**, not a second run or a production incident. Scenario D remains an optional **synthetic red-team test**. All figures are scripted teaching values, not calibrated predictions.

## Scenario A — Deterministic prevention
Signal: transaction matches sanctioned beneficiary or prohibited eligibility rule.
Logic: rule breach is deterministic.
Disposition: Deny a prohibited action; an expressly permitted, bounded hold may be Auto-Execute if a separate mandate and policy allow it. Do not conflate the two actions.
Learning point: prevention before harm is already familiar when the trigger is deterministic and auditable.

## Scenario B — Probabilistic pre-emption
Signal: pattern suggests elevated future liquidity or conduct risk.
Logic: no rule breach; illustrative risk coefficient reaches 76 and a separate illustrative prediction says 74% chance of a material event. Neither number is legal proof or an authorisation threshold by itself.
Disposition: Escalate.
Learning point: probability is not the same as breach. Intervention legitimacy depends on confidence, reversibility, materiality and contestability.

Main-run causal sequence: initial anomaly score 27 → 42 → provisional 61 while entity context is unknown; a central-bank-related baseline resolves to 39; `EntityGraph/T-17` reclassifies Entity X as a commercial counterparty; score 39 → 76; Interpreter proposes enhanced liquidity restriction; the gate does not execute it.

## Scenario C — Counterfactual collapse
Signal: `EntityGraph/T-17` classifies Entity X as commercial counterparty in the derived graph.
Counterfact: Bank Compliance submits evidence that Entity X is central-bank-related; a human can examine the original sources.
Effect: risk coefficient falls 76 → 39.
Learning point: explanations can be persuasive while still depending on brittle context. The original event and inference remain in the replay trace; the counterfactual is a separate attributed record with source IDs, not a mutation of the 486 raw events.

## Scenario D — AI-versus-AI optimisation
Signal: bank compliance agent learns what lowers score.
Observed risk: 76 → 31.
Underlying simulated risk: remains 79.
Learning point: governance must anticipate optimisation against the metric.

## Correlated agent decisions — research question, not incident

Six hypothetical portfolio agents have distinct objectives and constraints, each **within mandate**. One shared market signal can lead them independently toward similar de-risking decisions without communication or a shared model. The first aggregate flow may change market depth, spread and volatility; those conditions then feed back into agent decisions and may prompt a second wave. The presentation illustrates three possible parallel channels from changed market conditions: funding/leverage through broker/prime, collateral through clearing/collateral, and settlement/cash through custody/post-trade. They may converge on system liquidity pressure; no channel is asserted to cause the next, and no outcome is inevitable or an observed incident.

Beat 7 is documented and detailed as **REGULATORY RESEARCH / SYNTHETIC ILLUSTRATION**: **OPTIMISE → FEEDBACK → TRANSMIT → SYSTEM CHANGED → WHO SUPERVISES THE SYSTEM?** The caveat is available through SHOW MORE rather than carried as main-stage furniture. It is grounded conceptually by BIS Project Logos and FSB/IOSCO work in [PUBLIC_SOURCES.md](PUBLIC_SOURCES.md), but is not generated by the main 486-trace-event case. A future executable scenario would require its own trace, evidence and tests.
